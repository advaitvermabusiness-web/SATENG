---
name: SAT context-question standard
description: Product rules for generating Digital SAT-style words-in-context questions.
---

SAT words-in-context questions must be passage-driven rather than isolated definition recall. Rotate content across Literature, Science, Humanities, and Social Science, using advanced secondary to early undergraduate prose.

**Why:** The user wants SAT practice to model modern context-driven assessment, where transitions, contrast, and semantic signals determine one precise answer.

**How to apply:** Keep passages between 50 and 150 words, provide four in-scope options, and explain both why the correct choice fits and why each distractor fails.

Use three SAT context tiers without replacing earlier formats:
- Tier 1: the original short sentence with a word blank.
- Tier 2: 50–150-word academic passages with clear structural clues.
- Tier 3: denser academic passages with competing interpretations, qualifications, and subtler distinctions.

**Why:** The user wants increasing difficulty while preserving both previously approved context-question formats.

**How to apply:** Tier changes should affect SAT context questions only; keep the existing SAT meaning and precise-usage checks available in every tier.
