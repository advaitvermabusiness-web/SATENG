Here is a comprehensive, grouped list of high-yield advanced SAT words categorized by their common thematic usage on the exam.
## 🔄 Words Related to Change, Agreement, & Contrast
These words are highly frequent in reading passages that contrast two arguments or track an evolution in a scientific or historical narrative.

* Abate (v.): To reduce, lessen, or diminish in intensity.
* Ameliorate (v.): To make a bad or difficult situation better.
* Aquiesce (v.): To accept something reluctantly but without protest.
* Anachronism (n.): Something belonging to a period other than that in which it exists.
* Concur (v.): To be of the same opinion; to agree.
* Disparate (adj.): Essentially different in kind; not allowing comparison.
* Evanescent (adj.): Soon passing out of sight, memory, or existence; quickly fading.
* Immutable (adj.): Unchanging over time or unable to be changed.
* Malleable (adj.): Easily influenced; pliable or able to be shaped.
* Oscillate (v.): To move or swing back and forth at a regular speed.
* Spurious (adj.): Not being what it purports to be; false or fake.
* Transient (adj.): Lasting only for a short time; impermanent.

## 🧠 Words Related to Intellect, Knowledge, & Clarity
Use these words when evaluating authors' arguments, hypotheses, or clarity of expression in the text.

* Arcane (adj.): Understood by few; mysterious or secret.
* Cogent (adj.): Clear, logical, and convincing (used to describe an argument).
* Didactic (adj.): Intended to teach, particularly in having moral instruction as an ulterior motive.
* Elucidate (v.): To make something clear; to explain.
* Equivocal (adj.): Open to more than one interpretation; ambiguous or vague.
* Erudite (adj.): Having or showing great knowledge or learning.
* Inscrutable (adj.): Impossible to understand or interpret.
* Lucid (adj.): Expressed clearly; easy to understand.
* Nebulous (adj.): Unclear, vague, or ill-defined.
* Perspicacious (adj.): Having a ready insight into and understanding of things.
* Pragmatic (adj.): Dealing with things sensibly and realistically based on practical considerations.
* Sagacious (adj.): Having or showing keen mental discernment and good judgment.

## 🎭 Words Related to Emotion, Attitude, & Criticism
These terms often appear in questions asking about the author's tone, a character's stance, or a researcher's perspective.

* Ambivalent (adj.): Having mixed feelings or contradictory ideas about something or someone.
* Apathy (n.): Lack of interest, enthusiasm, or concern.
* Cynical (adj.): Believing that people are motivated purely by self-interest.
* Deride (v.): To express contempt for; to ridicule or mock.
* Disdain (n./v.): The feeling that someone or something is unworthy of one's consideration or respect.
* Dispassionate (adj.): Not influenced by strong emotion, and so able to be rational and impartial.
* Ebullient (adj.): Cheerful and full of energy.
* Indignant (adj.): Feeling or showing anger or annoyance at what is perceived as unfair treatment.
* Lament (v.): To express passionate grief or regret.
* Partisan (adj.): Prejudiced in favor of a particular cause or side.
* Supercilious (adj.): Behaving or looking as though one thinks one is superior to others.
* Venerate (v.): To regard with great respect; to revere.

## 📊 Words Related to Quantity, Scarcity, & Excess
These words appear regularly in scientific data interpretations or passages describing historical wealth and resources.

* Dearth (n.): A scarcity or lack of something.
* Copious (adj.): Abundant in supply or quantity.
* Glut (n.): An excessively abundant supply of something.
* Meticulous (adj.): Showing great attention to detail; very careful and precise.
* Paucity (n.): The presence of something only in small or insufficient quantities or amounts.
* Prodigal (adj.): Wasteful or extravagant in spending or resources.
* Profuse (adj.): Plentiful; abundant.
* Scrupulous (adj.): Diligent, thorough, and extremely attentive to details.
* Superfluous (adj.): Unnecessary, especially through being more than enough.
